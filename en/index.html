00 · The challenge

Plenty of reports, no diagnosis.

The sales team of a professional cosmetics manufacturer sells to distributors, perfumery chains, stores, salons, and pharmacies across the country. The data existed, but it was scattered: master data in the ERP, sales in BI reports, demonstrator schedules in custom tables, and an official revenue report used by the executive team. Every question became a new spreadsheet, and two spreadsheets rarely gave the same number.

“Who bought, who stopped, who grew, why, and where?”, answered in one place, with a number that matches the official one.

CUSTOMER BASE

Who is buying?

Active, new, reactivated, and lost customers (12 months without buying), plus who grew or declined compared with the previous year.

GROWTH

Where did the result come from?

Growth broken down by customer status, segment, state, sales rep, category, and the effect of price increases.

ACTION

Where to act?

Category penetration across customers, opportunities, stores with a demonstrator, and each sales rep's accounts, down to day-by-day detail.

01 · Principles

Before the code, the philosophy.

We agreed on a short set of principles with the business team. They settled dozens of discussions later on: whenever a question came up, the answer was already written down.

01

The business sets the rule; the data follows

Every rule (what counts as an active, lost, or new customer; what counts as an order) was defined by the sales team, documented, and applied the same way across the whole system.

02

All revenue counts

Nothing is silently discarded. A product without master data becomes “unclassified”; returns come in with a negative sign; employees count toward the total, but not as customers.

03

A customer is a trade name

Same trade name = same customer, in any branch and even with a new CNPJ (Brazilian company ID). When they really are different companies, the fix is made at the source (the master record), not with an exception in the code.

04

No number without a cross-check

Every view was validated against the official report before release. Any difference found gets a written explanation or a fix.

05

From macro to micro

The page tells a top-down story: headline indicators → customer base movement → growth breakdown → segments → states → sales reps → products.

06

Every number is a filter

Clicking any bar, slice, state, row, or card filters the whole page. Filters stack up, show as badges, and clear with one click.

02 · Where the data lives

Mapping the sources inside the system.

The first deliverable was not code: it was an inventory. For each business question, we located the table that answers it in the ERP (TOTVS Protheus) or in the BI tool, understood its grain (what one row represents), and noted each one's pitfall. Those pitfalls became the rules in chapter 04.

04 · Structure and rules

Clean without losing anything: every exclusion has a reason.

The customer master data is the heart of the diagnostic, and also the messiest source. The processing step separates what is a customer from what is not, without deleting anything: every record left out goes into a report with its reason and stays visible in the dashboard, in the “Cadastro geral” (full registry) table. Rules that became code

37-month rolling window

The most recent month in the sources defines the window: that month and the 36 before it. The current month is flagged as partial, and comparisons use the same period of the previous year.

Merging by trade name

Records with the same name (ignoring accents, spaces, and punctuation) become one customer, which inherits city, segment, and sales rep from the record with the most recent purchase.

Customer status

Active: bought in the period. Lost: reached 12 months without buying within the period. New or reactivated: no purchase in the previous 12 months. Grew or declined: versus the same period of the previous year.

Price increase

Current revenue ÷ (current units × previous year's price) − 1, using only SKUs sold in both periods. This way, price is not confused with volume or mix.

05 · Facts and dimensions

A star schema with the right grain.

The most important modeling decision was the grain of each fact. Revenue by product does not know how many orders there were, and the orders source does not know which products were bought. Instead of forcing a single table, we kept two facts with different grains, each responsible for the indicators it can sum without error, plus an event that links products to the day's purchase.

fato_faturamento_produto

Units, paid units (excluding free goods), and value. The source for revenue by product, category, average price, and price increase.

fato_pedidos_cliente

The only table where orders can be summed. Orders and average ticket per order come from here.

evento_compra_mix

Links products to the day's purchase (branch + customer + store + sales rep + date), to analyze mix and category penetration.

06 · The database

MongoDB Atlas as the analytical layer.

We chose MongoDB Atlas because it is easy to run in the cloud without a database server of our own, and because of its fast bulk loading. The collections follow the star schema, indexed on business keys and date fields. Besides the analytical tables, the database stores what gives the process operational safety:

controle_cargas

Every load is a batch

Records what was received, accepted, and rejected, the month window, and the publication status.

historico_importacoes

History with undo

Who imported, when, how long it took, the peak memory of each step, and a button to undo the last import.

usuarios_importacao

Protected access

Passwords hashed with scrypt and salt, lockout after 5 attempts, and email reset with a single-use link.

A load never replaces the published version before passing validation. If a source arrives in the wrong format, the dashboard keeps the previous load, and whoever imported it gets a plain-language message, such as “File open in another program” or “The customer file looks off-format”, instead of a technical error.

07 · Backend and loading

Eleven steps, one button.

The backend is plain Node.js, no framework: an HTTP server and load scripts with one responsibility each. The person importing only picks the files in the browser. The server validates the columns, chooses between a quick load (auxiliary master data only, about 3 minutes) and a full load (facts, about 20 minutes), runs the steps, and shows progress. 08 · Front-end

A dashboard built from scratch that computes in the browser.

No charting library, no framework. The server delivers a package with the dimensions and facts as binary vectors (base64-encoded integers). More than a million revenue rows fit in a few megabytes, and the calculation engine in the browser recomputes every indicator on each filter, with no round trip to the server.

// Exportação: cada coluna do fato vira um vetor tipado.
const F = {
  p:   new Uint8Array(n),   // período
  c:   new Uint16Array(n),  // cliente
  pr:  new Uint16Array(n),  // produto
  val: new Int32Array(n),   // valor em centavos
};

// No navegador: decodifica uma vez, recalcula a cada clique.
const val = new Int32Array(bytes(D.fato.val).buffer);

What the engine delivers

Each customer's status, a growth bridge (gains and losses by status), category penetration, price increase by SKU, accounts by sales rep, a state ranking, and day-by-day detail for a single customer.

All with automatic comparison against the same period of the previous year, or against the immediately preceding period.

10 · Validation

Match to the cent, or explain the difference.

Validation was treated as a deliverable, not as a final step. Every view was compared with the official report or with the BI tool, row by row, across all 37 months. The differences we found led to some of the project's best decisions:

What we validatedAgainstResultWhat we learned
Revenue by month and by sales repOfficial reportMatchesDifferences in the current month came only from extracts taken at different times.
OrdersBIMatchesThe BI tool counted the order number, which repeats across branches. The correct key is branch + number, and the right reference was the sum with the branch on the rows, not the consolidated total.
Segments and statesOfficial reportMatchesDifferences by state came from trade names repeated in different states. The fix was made in the master data, and the rule stayed simple.
Categories and productsBIMatchesEvery SKU matches in value and quantity; only the name of one technical category differs.
Customers with the largest growth and declineRaw dataMatchesChecked customer by customer against the original extracts.
Stores with a demonstratorBIDecisionThe BI tool linked demonstrators even to returns from stores with no schedule. The sales team chose the schedule as the source of truth, and the discrepancy was documented.

Validating is not proving the dashboard is right. It is finding where the sources disagree and leaving the decision to the people who know the business.

11 · 24/7 deployment

Online all day, for US$ 11 a month.

The dashboard moved from a local server to the cloud, with three requirements: access by link only (no login on the dashboard), imports from any computer, and no company data in the code repository.

A disk that vanishes on every deploy

The cloud server starts empty on every deploy. The source files and the dashboard package are mirrored in Atlas, compressed (601 MB becomes 49 MB). On startup, the server downloads whatever is missing; at the end of each load, it uploads what changed.

A machine sized by measurement, not estimate

Each load step records its own peak memory. The fact load reached about 1 GB. We tested a 1 GB machine with limited memory, it failed, and we chose the 2 GB one, with headroom for the data to grow.

23 MB that became 3.3 MB

The dashboard package is sent compressed and with an ETag. On a second visit, with no new load, the browser downloads nothing: it just gets “not modified” (304).

Secret link, no login

The dashboard only responds at /<chave>/. Outside of it, any address returns “Not found”. Headers prevent indexing and leaking the key through the Referer. If the link leaks, just rotate the key.

// Sem a chave no caminho, o servidor não revela nem que existe.
if (chaveAcesso) {
  const prefixo = `/${chaveAcesso}`;
  if (!rota.startsWith(`${prefixo}/`)) return naoEncontrado(response);
  rota = rota.slice(prefixo.length);

12 · This version

The same dashboard, with a company that does not exist.

This portfolio version runs entirely on GitHub Pages, with no database and no server. The dashboard code is the same as in the production version. Only the data package was swapped for a fictional dataset, created by a custom generator for Aurora Cosméticos Profissionais, a made-up manufacturer.

CUSTOMERS

1,822 records

Chains with several stores, the same store with a new CNPJ, employees, and invalid records.

SALES

169k rows

37 months with seasonality, annual price increases, free goods, and returns.

LIFECYCLE

Living customers

New, lost, and reactivated customers, and accounts changing hands between sales reps.

FIELD

46 demonstrators

Monthly schedules that increase purchases at the stores they cover.

The generator uses a fixed seed (the output is always the same) and writes the package in the exact format the real pipeline exports from the database. That is why every filter, click, map, table, the CSV export, and the theme work just as in production. No name, CNPJ, customer, sales rep, or value comes from real data.
